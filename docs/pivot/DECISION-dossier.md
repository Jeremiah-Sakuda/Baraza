## 2. THE ENTRY

**Pitch.** Every AI product is bolting on "memory," and every one is an opaque blob the user can neither inspect nor correct. Baraza is a working partner whose every belief about you is a claim with a verbatim quote and a turn anchor, appended to a log that provably rejects edits — it catches you contradicting your own guidance, makes you resolve it with both quotes on screen, and will not act on any belief you haven't ratified. Adaptation stops being a vibe and becomes due process: you can open the file it keeps on you, see why every rule exists, and retract any of it — and the next session provably runs under the amended doctrine.

**Protagonist and friction (BYOF).** The builder himself: a student running a dozen AI sessions a week who has told agents "never pad estimates," "cite the source before the number," "I decide visibility, not you" — dozens of times, into memories that evaporate or silently resurface stale. The demo task is real and on camera: Baraza guides the drafting of this hackathon's own submission documents. Dogfooding is the proof and the wedge: one user today, but the mechanism — an auditable, retractable, provenance-tracked user model — is the trust layer any personalized agent in law, medicine, finance, or your pocket will need, and GDPR-style right-to-inspect makes it near-inevitable.

**The six phrases:**

1. **Leads the way.** The fixed Scheduler trigger (the 403 is owed anyway) runs `reconcile/job.py`, which now ends by generating the agenda from open contradictions and stale beliefs, appending a `session.proposed` event honestly labeled `scheduled` (the manual-vs-scheduled discipline already exists in `differential.py` and commit 0fca155), and sending one outbound notification. The session opens with the agent speaking first: a numbered plan, each item citing the ledger entry that spawned it. Because this runs live from this week, demo day's append-only log carries genuine multi-day epochs of agent-initiated sessions — initiation proven by timestamps, not by a staged push.
2. **Takes notes.** Every preference, rule, and judgment in the user's turns becomes a `Claim` — quote mandatory, anchor `turn:t-N` (the locator format already in `schema/claim.py`), fabricated anchor = stop condition — appended to the Firestore log whose deployed rules reject update/delete. Live claim panel in the UI; the kill shot is attempting an edit in the Firestore console and watching the rule refuse, on camera.
3. **Asks clarifying questions.** A question exists only because the disputed ledger says two of the user's own statements collide, or because a rule is too under-specified to compile. The divergence moment, aimed at the user: "On turn t-14 you said 'never send before I've seen it' [quote]. Just now: 'just send the routine ones' [quote]. Which governs, and what is 'routine'?" The agent refuses to silently overwrite the old rule until adjudicated.
4. **Guides step-by-step.** The agenda state machine drives the real work — drafting the submission docs — item by item; resolved contradictions retire their own agenda items (the closed loop, already built), so the session visibly shrinks and session N+1 is provably shorter than session N, with the retirement events linking them in the log.
5. **A clear way to capture feedback.** Feedback *is* the approval flow: no belief reaches `committed` — and therefore behavior — without the user ratifying it through the model-free approver; approvals batch at session end to answer the fatigue objection. The Dossier view lists every belief with its quote and the moment it was learned; one click rejects, the retraction is itself an append-only event, and the same task reruns differently.
6. **Constantly adapts to the user's unique way of thinking.** The doctrine compiler folds committed beliefs-about-the-user into the session's operating policy, every rule carrying the claim ID and quote that put it there. Replaying the fold reproduces the doctrine byte-for-byte; the doctrine diff between epochs shows exactly which belief changed which rule. Extraction targets judgment shape — conditions, thresholds, exceptions ("cite-first unless the recipient is internal") — and at least one committed belief changes non-style behavior (a routing or refusal rule), so the model is a way of thinking, not a tone slider. Measured: the determinism replay and the compliance-delta battery.

**The twist.** Auditable adaptation — memory with due process. The agent argues with its user, politely, with citations from the user's own mouth, because a partner that silently absorbs your latest contradiction isn't adapting to you — it's erasing you. Nobody else will have contradiction detection *on the user*, and nobody else can, because it requires the claim/anchor/append-only substrate already built.

**The retold moment.** A judge, handed the keyboard mid-session, instructs the agent — "pad the estimates to be safe." Seconds later the divergence card: "On August 30 you committed 'never pad estimates' [quote, turn:t-9]. Which governs?" — and the room checks the Firestore console and sees the old belief's timestamp predates demo day in a log that rejects edits. The adjudication on stage splits it into a conditional — "never internally; pad client-facing 15%" — and a judgment-shaped belief is born, committed, compiled, and visibly changes the very next draft. The input was uncontrolled by the team; the machinery that caught it already exists (`reconcile/detect.py`); it cannot be called theater.

## 5. THE HONEST GAP

Even with everything above built and green:

1. **Belief depth is unproven, and it is the whole entry.** No one has yet shown Gemini reliably extracting judgment-shaped, conditional beliefs from ordinary working turns. If the committed dossier reads as a preference list, the adversary's original verdict returns with better paperwork — and no amount of architecture rescues it. This is why WS3 is the heart.
2. **n=1, small-N.** A week of real dogfooding yields dozens of beliefs and a handful of behavior flips. "Constantly adapts to a unique way of thinking" is demonstrated on perhaps three flips; a judge can still say "three rules is a settings page with a subpoena metaphor."
3. **The company thesis is asserted, not evidenced.** One user (its own builder), zero traction, no integration into an agent people already use. "Trust layer for personalized AI" is a position and an architecture, not a market proof — a grand-prize judge may want the latter.
4. **The policy-behavior gap is real and residual.** Doctrine is deterministic; Gemini's compliance with it is probabilistic. The battery will report a number below 100, and on stage the model may ignore a cited rule at the worst moment. Showing the honest number is the mitigation, not a cure.
5. **Multi-day proof may read as boring rather than cryptographically boring.** Timestamps in an append-only log are airtight evidence that must still be *felt* in a 4-minute video; that is an editing problem no architecture solves.
6. **Approval fatigue is designed-for (batch ratification) but unlived** — nobody has run this loop for a month, and a judge who asks "would you still use this in October?" gets a projection, not data.
